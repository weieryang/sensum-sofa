Compressed Sofa Compression and Recovery Test Protocol

Published: 2026-08-27 · Category: Packing, Recovery & Sample Approval

A recovery claim is useful only when the buyer knows which model was tested, how it was compressed, how long it remained packed, when it was opened and what was measured. This protocol turns an unpacking demonstration into a traceable approval record for samples and bulk-order planning.

Start by defining what the recovery test must prove

The test should answer a commercial question, not only produce an attractive video. An importer may need to confirm that the product reaches the approved selling condition after the expected logistics cycle, that dimensions remain within the agreed model tolerance, or that the unboxing instructions are realistic for a warehouse or end customer.

Write the objective before testing. Identify the model, configuration, fabric route, packing method and expected destination channel. Connect the plan to the compressed sofa specification sheet so the test does not approve a different version from the quotation or sample.

Buyer rule: There is no useful recovery result without a named model, defined test conditions, fixed observation points and written acceptance criteria.

Record the approved condition before compression

The pre-compression sample is the reference against which later observations are compared. Let the sample reach its normal condition, assemble it correctly and place it on a level surface. Use consistent lighting and camera positions so later photos show real differences rather than a change in angle.

Control and document the packed conditions

Recovery is affected by the full test route. Record the compression date, equipment or method used, inner protection, sealed package, carton dimensions and the date the carton is opened. If the test is intended to represent shipping and storage, document where and how the carton was stored instead of reporting only the number of days.

Compression record

Date, method, operators, package condition and photos before the carton is closed.

Carton record

External dimensions, package count, labels and model identification.

Storage record

Start and end dates, location, orientation and notable environmental conditions.

Chain of identity

Evidence that the unpacked unit is the same sample that entered the test.

Run timed recovery checks without moving the goalposts

Before opening the carton, agree the observation schedule. The schedule may include an immediate record, intermediate checkpoints and one final decision point recommended for the selected model. Do not choose a convenient passing time after seeing the result.

Step 1. Film the complete unpacking

Show the carton label, seal condition, opening method, removal of protection and the moment the product is placed in its recovery position. Record the start time and avoid editing out handling steps that could affect the result.

Step 2. Use the same photo positions

At every checkpoint, capture the same front, side, rear and detail views used for the baseline. Keep the sofa on the same type of surface and note any manual shaping allowed by the approved instructions.

Step 3. Repeat the defined measurements

Measure only at the points named in the specification. Record the value, time and person taking the measurement. Upholstered furniture can vary, so use the model-specific tolerance agreed with the supplier rather than inventing a universal percentage.

Step 4. Record condition, not only dimensions

Check shape symmetry, fabric creasing, seam position, module alignment, arm and back presentation, seat support and any odor or handling issue relevant to the target channel.

Step 5. Complete the final review at the agreed time

Compare the unit with the baseline, approved sample and acceptance fields. A result should be marked pass, accepted with an agreed observation, retest required or fail, with the responsible approver named.

Turn the test into an order decision

A sample test should define what happens next. If the model passes, attach the record to the approved sample and production specification. If the result is unclear, identify whether the issue came from the product, packing method, packed duration, storage, unpacking or measurement process before changing the design.

Copy-ready recovery evidence template

  1. Test identity: project, model, revision, sample ID, fabric and packing method.
  2. Baseline: dimensions, condition notes, dated photos and specification reference.
  3. Packing: compression date, package count, carton dimensions, labels and packing photos.
  4. Storage: packed duration, location, orientation and recorded conditions.
  5. Unpacking: opening date and time, complete video, instructions followed and handlers.
  6. Timed checks: checkpoint time, measurements, photos, condition notes and permitted shaping.
  7. Final decision: acceptance criteria, result, deviations, approver and next action.

For sample development, keep this file with the sample approval checklist. For shipment release, connect it to the pre-shipment inspection checklist.

Compressed sofa recovery test FAQ

Is there one universal recovery time for every compressed sofa?

No. Recovery depends on the model, construction, materials, compression method, packed duration and environmental conditions. Approve a model-specific final checkpoint with the supplier.

What should be measured before a sofa is compressed?

Record the approved sample identity, overall and seat dimensions, visible shape, comfort reference, fabric condition and dated photos from defined angles.

What evidence should buyers keep during unpacking?

Keep the carton and label record, packed dates, complete opening video, unpacking start time, timed photos, measurements, condition notes and the final comparison.

Should a recovery test simulate the expected shipping and storage period?

The plan should reflect the intended logistics cycle as closely as practical. Record the actual packed duration and conditions so the result remains traceable.

Can one recovery test approve every size and fabric in a sofa series?

Not automatically. Confirm whether changes in size, construction, foam, upholstery or packing require separate evidence or an updated approval record.
