Compressed Sofa Product Specification Sheet Template

Published: 2026-08-21 · Category: Product Specification & OEM Control

A quotation tells you the price. A sample shows one physical result. A product specification sheet connects both to the repeatable requirements used for purchasing, production, inspection and shipment. This guide shows importers what to put in that file before a compressed sofa order moves into bulk production.

Why importers need one controlled sofa specification

Compressed sofa projects combine upholstery, foam, structural support and vacuum packing. If these details are approved in separate messages, the buyer may receive a correct-looking sample but an incomplete bulk-order standard. The risk increases when colors, seat counts, carton artwork or marketplace labels vary across several SKUs.

A controlled specification sheet gives every party the same reference. The buyer uses it to compare quotations and approve revisions. The supplier uses it to prepare materials and production instructions. The inspection company uses it to verify measurable requirements and packing evidence. The warehouse and after-sales team can later identify what was actually ordered.

Buyer rule: If a requirement can affect price, appearance, recovery, labeling or shipment acceptance, record it in the approved specification instead of leaving it only in chat history.

The sheet does not need to expose confidential production know-how. It needs enough clear information to identify the approved product, measure the result and prevent quotation assumptions from changing unnoticed.

How to define sofa dimensions and tolerances

Upholstered furniture contains soft materials, so buyers should not treat dimensions like machined metal parts. At the same time, writing only an approximate size is not enough for marketplace listings, room planning, carton design or inspection. The practical solution is to define how a dimension is measured and agree a model-specific acceptance range.

State the condition

Record whether measurements are taken after assembly and after the agreed recovery period.

Show the measuring points

Use a drawing or annotated photo so width, depth, height and seat dimensions are repeatable.

Agree each tolerance

Set the acceptance range with the supplier by model. Do not assume one universal tolerance for every sofa.

Control the revision

If dimensions change, update the quotation, carton data, listing information and approved drawing together.

A practical specification approval workflow

Step 1. Open the file with the RFQ

Enter the buyer requirement, target model, quantity, destination and packing limits before requesting the quotation. Mark unknown items as pending instead of allowing suppliers to make invisible assumptions.

Step 2. Add supplier confirmations

Record the offered model, material route, carton reference, MOQ and any deviations from the brief. If two suppliers propose different assumptions, the difference should be visible in the comparison.

Step 3. Update the sheet during sampling

Replace estimates with approved references: measured dimensions, signed fabric swatches, sample photos, recovery notes and the final accessory list.

Step 4. Freeze a production revision

Give the approved sheet a revision number and date. Both parties should know which document authorizes material purchasing and bulk production.

Step 5. Inspect against the same revision

Send the inspection team the approved requirements and evidence list. If a change is accepted after production starts, document the deviation and approval rather than silently editing the original file.

Common specification-sheet mistakes

  1. Using a marketing product name without a unique model, configuration and revision.
  2. Writing dimensions without defining the product condition or measuring points.
  3. Approving a fabric color by screen image instead of an exact swatch or sample reference.
  4. Keeping carton and barcode requirements in a separate chat that production cannot trace.
  5. Using subjective phrases such as "high density," "strong frame" or "good quality" without an approved reference.
  6. Changing one field without checking its effect on quotation, packing, listing data or inspection.
  7. Letting the physical sample replace all written product and packing requirements.

Compressed sofa specification FAQ

What should a compressed sofa specification sheet include?

It should identify the exact model and approved sample, dimensions, construction and materials, fabric and color, accessories, packing data, labels, recovery instructions, inspection checkpoints and approval owners.

Should sofa dimension tolerances be included in the specification?

Yes. Agree how each dimension is measured and what tolerance applies to that model. Do not rely on an unstated universal tolerance for upholstered products.

When should the specification sheet be approved?

Start the working sheet with the RFQ and update it during sampling. Approve the production revision before materials are released for bulk production.

Can the approved sample replace a written sofa specification?

No. The sample provides physical evidence, while the written sheet identifies the revision, measurable requirements, packing and inspection criteria needed for repeat orders.

How should specification changes be controlled after approval?

Use a revision number and change log. Record what changed, why, who approved it and which quotation, sample, carton or inspection document also needs updating.
