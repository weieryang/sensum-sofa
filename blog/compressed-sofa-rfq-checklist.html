Compressed Sofa RFQ Checklist for Importers

Published: 2026-06-14 · Category: RFQ Preparation & Supplier Communication

A fast quotation starts with a complete inquiry. If the first RFQ only says "please send price," most suppliers will reply with generic information or ask several rounds of follow-up questions. Buyers get better quotations when they send the right commercial, product and packing details in the first message.

Why a complete RFQ matters for compressed sofa sourcing

Compressed sofa quotations depend on more than style and seat count. The supplier needs enough information to match the correct model, confirm the packing method, estimate freight efficiency and understand whether the order is a stock-style program or an OEM project. Without that context, the first quotation may not reflect the real landed-cost risk.

For B2B buyers, a better RFQ means fewer quotation revisions, cleaner MOQ discussions and faster decisions about sampling, packaging and compliance. It also makes supplier comparison more useful because the same data is being quoted by each factory.

If you do not know the final model yet, send a reference photo and explain the style, dimensions and target price range. That is still better than asking for a quotation with no product direction.

Practical rule: Ask for the quotation together with packing data. Buyers should not separate unit price from carton size, package count, gross weight and CBM when comparing compressed sofa suppliers.

Questions that help the supplier give a more useful first reply

  1. Which existing model is closest to my reference image or market target?
  2. What are the finished dimensions, carton dimensions, package count and CBM?
  3. What is the MOQ by model, fabric and color?
  4. Which fabric options are available for this price level?
  5. Can the project include custom labels, carton marks or barcodes?
  6. What is the sample timeline and what is the bulk lead time after approval?
  7. Which reports or documents may be relevant to my destination market?

What the supplier should send back before you compare quotations

A quotation is easier to compare when each supplier provides the same structure. Buyers should request model basics, packing details and commercial terms in one answer rather than collecting them across separate emails.

Product data

Model number, finished dimensions, material summary and available variations.

Packing data

Carton size, package count, gross weight, net weight, CBM and loading reference.

Commercial terms

MOQ, quotation validity, sample cost, sample lead time and bulk lead time.

OEM scope

Which changes are possible for fabric, labels, manuals, barcodes and carton artwork.

Quality evidence

Sample photos, unpacking photos, packing photos or inspection checkpoints.

Compliance scope

Available certificates or declarations relevant to the buyer's target market.

Use the product catalog to shortlist models, the packing page to check how loading data affects freight planning, and the fabric page when the quotation depends on shared color or material programs.

How importers should compare sofa quotations

Do not compare quotations only on ex-factory or FOB price. A lower price with incomplete packing data can create more freight cost or rework later. Buyers should line up each quotation by the same fields:

Comparison areaWhat to check
Model accuracyIs the quoted sofa actually the same structure, seat count and size?
Packing efficiencyWhich quotation has the better carton size, package count and loading potential?
MOQ and color rulesAre MOQ limits different by fabric, color or OEM packaging?
Lead timeWas the timeline quoted for sample approval only, or also for bulk production?
OEM impactDid custom labels, barcode or carton changes already get counted in the quote?
Evidence qualityWhich supplier shared clearer packing photos, recovery notes or specification detail?

Common RFQ mistakes that slow down sourcing

  1. Sending only a product photo without quantity, destination or packing needs.
  2. Comparing quotations before carton size, package count and CBM are confirmed.
  3. Waiting until after quotation approval to mention OEM labels or carton artwork.
  4. Using one general MOQ number for all colors and materials.
  5. Asking for compliance support without naming the destination market or sales channel.
  6. Approving a quotation before the sample and written specification match each other.

Buyer FAQ

What should I include in a compressed sofa RFQ?

Include model number or reference photo, quantity, destination country, preferred fabric or color, packing requirements, target market, OEM label or carton needs and your target launch timing. This gives the supplier enough context to recommend the correct model and packing method.

Do I need product photos if I do not know the exact model number?

Yes. A reference photo, rough dimensions and intended category help the supplier match the right product family before quoting. If you are still choosing between categories, mention whether you are comparing compressed sofa, sofa bed or modular sofa options.

Why should importers request carton size and CBM in the first quotation?

Carton size, package count, gross weight and CBM affect freight cost, warehouse handling and loading efficiency. Buyers who only compare unit price may miss the real shipping difference between two similar-looking quotations.

When should OEM labels, manuals and carton artwork be discussed?

Discuss them in the first RFQ or immediately after the first quotation, because OEM packaging changes can affect MOQ, lead time, approval steps and final cost. The certificate page and OEM / ODM page help frame those conversations early.
