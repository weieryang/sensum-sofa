What AQL inspection means for a compressed sofa order

AQL inspection gives buyers a structured way to assess a production lot without opening and checking every carton. The lot size, inspection level and agreed AQL values are used with a sampling plan to determine how many units to inspect and which acceptance or rejection numbers apply. Inspectors then record each nonconformity against a defect category.

The current international reference is ISO 2859-1:2026, published in January 2026. It defines AQL-indexed sampling schemes for lot-by-lot inspection. Buyers should obtain the applicable standard or work with a qualified inspection provider when selecting the formal sample plan.

Important limit: An AQL pass does not mean every sofa is defect-free. It means the inspected sample met the agreed acceptance rule. Safety, regulatory, functional and contractual hold points still need separate review.

1. Make four decisions before the inspector selects cartons

Buyer decisionWhat must be agreedRisk if left unclear
Lot definitionWhich PO, SKU, color, production batch and finished quantity form the inspection lotThe sample may not represent the goods being released
Inspection planApplicable standard, inspection level, AQL values and normal, tightened or other agreed statusBuyer and inspector may calculate different sample or decision rules
Defect listProduct-specific critical, major and minor examples tied to the approved specificationThe same issue may be graded differently after it is found

Common furniture AQL values may be useful as a discussion starting point, but they are not a universal rule for every sofa, market or selling channel. The buyer should state the selected values in the PO, quality agreement or inspection instruction instead of relying on an inspector's default.

2. Define critical, major and minor defects for the exact sofa

Defect classification should be based on consequence, not only appearance. A loose thread and an unstable seat are both nonconformities, but they create very different customer and safety risks. The approved sample, specification, destination requirements and retail promise should determine the final grade.

Defect classPractical meaningCompressed sofa examples
CriticalPotential safety hazard, prohibited condition or serious compliance riskExposed sharp staple or metal, unstable load-bearing structure, collapse risk, prohibited contamination, or missing mandatory safety information where applicable
MajorLikely to affect function, agreed specification, normal sale or customer acceptanceWrong model or fabric, open seam, broken zipper, missing component, severe stain or hole, dimension outside agreed tolerance, failed recovery after the agreed method, or incorrect fulfillment label
MinorLimited cosmetic deviation that does not materially affect normal useSmall loose thread, faint removable mark, slight non-prominent stitch variation, or minor carton scuff that does not affect protection or label readability
Classification rule: The same visual issue can change grade by location, size, frequency and retail channel. Define measurable boundaries and reference photos before production whenever possible.

3. Add compressed-sofa checkpoints to the generic AQL list

A generic furniture checklist will miss compression-specific risks. The inspector should still verify workmanship, dimensions and labels, but the order file also needs instructions for packing condition, controlled unpacking and recovery evidence.

Identity and specification

Match the SKU, configuration, fabric, color, dimensions and accessories to the approved sample and written product file.

Structure and function

Check stability, load-bearing condition, seams, zippers, connectors, detachable parts and any model-specific operation.

Appearance and workmanship

Review visible surfaces, stitching, stains, holes, shape consistency and color against the agreed viewing conditions.

Compression and recovery

Record carton condition, unpacking time, room conditions and recovery at the model-specific checkpoints agreed by buyer and supplier.

Carton and fulfillment

Verify package count, carton integrity, barcode, SKU label, handling mark and destination-specific packing instructions.

Quantity and lot traceability

Connect every inspected unit and defect photo to the carton, SKU and production lot used in the release decision.

4. Run the inspection in a sequence that preserves evidence

  1. Freeze the finished lot and confirm the PO, SKU mix and available quantity before sample selection.
  2. Select cartons randomly across locations, batches and colors according to the agreed sampling instruction.
  3. Photograph carton condition, labels and seals before opening the selected packages.
  4. Check identity, dimensions, workmanship, function and accessories against the approved files.
  5. Follow the agreed unpacking and recovery method for any compression-related assessment.
  6. Record each defect once, assign the agreed class and connect it to the exact unit and image.
  7. Compare totals with the applicable acceptance and rejection numbers, then document non-AQL findings separately.

The broader pre-shipment inspection checklist covers quantity, carton marks and loading readiness. This AQL guide adds the sampling and defect-classification layer used to judge the production lot.

5. Turn the inspection report into a controlled disposition

DispositionWhen it may applyEvidence to close
ReleaseSample meets the agreed rule and no unresolved safety, specification or shipment hold existsSigned report, final quantity, carton data and approval owner
Conditional acceptanceBuyer accepts a defined deviation with a written commercial or operational conditionWaiver scope, affected quantity, credit or correction and owner approval
Rework and reinspectionCorrectable defects exceed the rule or repeat across the inspected sampleRoot cause, rework quantity, corrected photos and new random inspection result
Hold or rejectCritical finding, serious mismatch, failed reinspection or unresolved commercial riskSegregated lot, defect evidence, corrective plan and buyer decision

Do not delete the original report after rework. Keep the first findings, corrective action and reinspection under the same PO and lot history. If defects are discovered after arrival, move the evidence into the warranty claim form and evidence pack so the supplier can compare the field case with pre-shipment records.

6. Common AQL inspection mistakes buyers should avoid

  1. Writing only "AQL inspection required" without naming the plan, values or defect definitions.
  2. Letting the inspector classify defects without the approved sample, specification and buyer tolerance.
  3. Selecting convenient cartons from one location instead of a representative random sample.
  4. Counting defects without recording which unit, carton, SKU and photo show each finding.
  5. Treating an AQL pass as a substitute for safety, functional, recovery or compliance checks.
  6. Accepting factory rework without a defined quantity, corrective evidence and reinspection rule.

FAQ

What does AQL mean in a compressed sofa inspection?

AQL is used with an agreed sampling plan to select a sample and compare observed defect counts with acceptance and rejection numbers for the lot. It does not mean every unit has been inspected or guarantee a defect-free shipment.

How should compressed sofa defects be classified?

Buyers normally define critical defects as safety or compliance risks, major defects as problems likely to affect function, specification or saleability, and minor defects as limited cosmetic deviations. The final defect list should be agreed before inspection.

Does an AQL pass automatically mean the shipment should be released?

No. Buyers should also review critical findings, functional and safety checks, packaging, quantity, specification mismatches and any contractual hold points before authorizing shipment.
