How to Import Compressed Sofas from China

Published: 2026-06-13 · Category: Import Planning & Supplier Communication

Importing compressed sofas from China is not only about finding a low unit price. Buyers need a clear process for model selection, sample approval, MOQ, compliance, packing data, shipment timing and issue handling before they commit to a bulk order.

Why import compressed sofas from China?

Compressed sofas can reduce carton volume and make container loading more efficient than traditional packed sofas. For wholesalers, online furniture brands and importers working with warehouse limits, better packing efficiency can improve freight planning and inventory turnover. China is also a common sourcing base for buyers who want access to multiple sofa styles, OEM options and established export workflows.

That advantage only works when the supplier can provide accurate carton data, product specifications and realistic lead-time commitments. If the product recovers poorly after unpacking or the shipment data is incomplete, the expected freight savings may disappear.

A practical 7-step import process for B2B buyers

Step 1. Define the commercial target before requesting quotations

Clarify who will sell the sofa, which market it is for, whether the order is for retail, wholesale or e-commerce, and what price band the product must hit. This determines whether you need a basic stock model, a new OEM configuration or a more customized private-label program. Use the product catalog to shortlist the correct models before asking for price.

Step 2. Request complete quotation data, not only FOB price

For each model, ask for finished size, carton size, gross weight, package count, CBM, 40HQ loading reference and available fabric options. Freight cost is affected by volume, not only by product price, so incomplete packing data creates avoidable risk. The packing guide for importers shows why carton information belongs in the first round of evaluation.

Step 3. Approve the sample and written specification together

A sample should confirm comfort, appearance, seam quality, fabric choice, label position and recovery condition after unpacking. At the same time, ask for a written specification covering size, fabric, foam, support layers, packing method and carton details. The sample without a locked specification is incomplete; the specification without a sample is also risky.

Step 4. Confirm MOQ and customization scope early

MOQ can change when you change fabric, color, label, carton artwork or component structure. Discuss whether your project uses an existing model or a true OEM program. The OEM/ODM page is a useful starting point for discussing private-label cartons, barcodes and custom dimensions.

Step 5. Check compliance for the destination market

Import requirements depend on the destination country, materials, sales channel and product category. Ask which reports, declarations or test files are available for the exact model and market, then confirm whether they still match your product configuration. Review the available certificate library, but do not assume one document automatically covers every SKU or country.

Step 6. Lock inspection and shipment checkpoints before mass production

Define what the supplier must confirm before shipment: final product photos, carton photos, labels, package count, loading estimate and pre-shipment inspection points. Importers should know who checks fabric color, carton mark, quantity and packing condition before the container is booked.

Step 7. Keep the final order file complete

Your final order file should include the approved sample reference, product specification, packing method, carton dimensions, labeling rules, quantity, destination and agreed lead time. This written package helps both sides manage revisions and reduces disputes after shipment.

Importer rule: If the supplier cannot provide stable packing data, recovery expectations and written OEM details, the project is not ready for bulk order regardless of how attractive the first quotation looks.

How importers should think about lead time

Lead time is more than the factory production window. Buyers should separate the project into quotation, sample confirmation, material approval, bulk production, inspection, booking and ocean transit. If any one of these stages is vague, the schedule becomes unreliable.

Quotation stage

Model selection, initial packing data, target quantity and destination are confirmed.

Sample stage

Appearance, comfort, fabric, label position and recovery condition are approved.

Bulk stage

Production starts only after deposit, final specification and material approval are clear.

Inspection stage

Final quantity, carton marking, package quality and shipment readiness are checked.

Booking stage

Container booking and loading plan should match the final carton data.

Arrival stage

Plan for customs, inland transport, unloading and recovery time after unpacking.

If your sales plan has a fixed launch date, work backward from the arrival target rather than trusting a single general lead-time statement.

Common import mistakes to avoid

  1. Comparing suppliers only on unit price while ignoring CBM, carton size and gross weight.
  2. Skipping sample approval when the order includes new fabric, custom dimensions or private-label packaging.
  3. Using generic compliance files without confirming they match the ordered model and market.
  4. Accepting a loading estimate without the carton calculation behind it.
  5. Leaving recovery expectations, inspection points or replacement terms undocumented.
  6. Waiting until production is underway to finalize logos, labels, carton artwork or barcode details.

Buyer FAQ

What information should I send before asking for a compressed sofa quotation?

Send the model number or product photo, quantity, destination country, target price range, preferred fabric or color, packing requirements and whether you need OEM labels or carton artwork. This helps the supplier recommend the correct model and calculate more realistic packing data.

What is the normal MOQ for imported compressed sofas?

There is no universal MOQ. MOQ depends on the model, fabric, color mix, packaging and whether the order uses an existing specification or a customized OEM project. Ask for MOQ by model and color, not only one general factory MOQ.

How can importers reduce risk before placing a bulk sofa order?

Approve the sample, written specification, packing method, carton data, inspection points, compliance scope and production timeline before releasing the bulk order. Written confirmation is more reliable than verbal promises.

Why is carton data important when importing sofas from China?

Carton size, gross weight, CBM and loading quantity directly affect freight cost, warehouse handling and whether the product fits your fulfillment plan. Without that data, it is difficult to compare true landed cost between suppliers.
