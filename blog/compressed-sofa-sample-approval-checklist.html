Why sample approval matters in compressed sofa sourcing

Compressed sofa projects carry two kinds of risk at the same time: product risk and packing risk. Buyers are not only checking whether the sofa looks good on day one. They also need to know whether the product recovers properly after unpacking, whether the chosen fabric matches the target market, and whether the packing method still supports the intended freight plan.

That is why sample approval should never be treated as a single yes-or-no reaction to the showroom look. The sample stage is where buyers validate the full reference for bulk production. The import planning guide explains the wider sourcing process, while this page focuses on how to approve the sample correctly.

Importer rule: A compressed sofa sample is not fully approved until the product, packing data and written specification all match one another.

A practical sample approval workflow for B2B buyers

Step 1. Define the exact sample purpose before production starts

Clarify whether the sample is for comfort review, fabric approval, private label presentation, carton validation or a full pre-bulk reference. The answer determines which details must be signed off and which can wait.

Step 2. Review the sample after normal recovery, not only right after unpacking

Compressed sofas can change appearance as the filling recovers. Ask how long the sample should rest before evaluation, then review it again after the recommended recovery window. This is especially important for projects with larger seat surfaces or soft foam structures.

Step 3. Compare the physical sample with the written specification

Check that the approved sample matches the dimension sheet, fabric code, packing method and label plan. If the sample looks correct but the specification is incomplete, the bulk order can still drift. Buyers should request both the sample and the written file in the same approval cycle.

Step 4. Confirm packing data before releasing the bulk order

Do not wait until shipment planning to ask about carton size or gross weight. Sample approval is the right stage to confirm package count, CBM and loading assumptions, because those figures influence landed cost and warehouse planning.

Step 5. Record the approval in one final sign-off package

Save the approved sample photos, dimension sheet, fabric code, packing data, label notes and any recovery comments in one file. That package is what the supplier, sourcing team and inspection team should use as the bulk-order reference.

Common sample approval mistakes to avoid

  1. Approving comfort and appearance without checking the final carton data.
  2. Reviewing the sofa only immediately after unpacking instead of after the agreed recovery time.
  3. Allowing the bulk order to proceed before the written specification is finalized.
  4. Changing fabric, label or carton rules after the sample is already signed off.
  5. Using one sample to approve several model variations without a clear SKU list.
  6. Skipping approval photos and notes, then relying on memory during mass production.

What buyers should sign off in writing

Sample photos

Front, side, back, close-up details and recovery photos after unpacking.

Specification sheet

Model code, dimensions, fabric code, material summary and packing method.

Packing data

Carton size, package count, gross weight, net weight, CBM and loading estimate.

Approval notes

Label position, barcode rules, carton marks, acceptable tolerance and follow-up actions.

If the project also needs destination-market files, attach the relevant certificate or declaration notes to the same sample approval package so compliance is not separated from product approval.

Questions to send your supplier today

  1. How long should the sample recover before the final appearance review?
  2. Which dimensions and materials are already fixed, and which can still change?
  3. Can the supplier provide carton size, gross weight, CBM and package count with the sample review?
  4. What labels, barcode rules or carton marks must be confirmed before bulk production?
  5. Which photos and documents will be used as the final bulk-order reference?
  6. Does any fabric, label or packing change require a new sample approval?

Buyer FAQ

What should importers check on a compressed sofa sample before approval?

Check dimensions, seat comfort, recovery after unpacking, fabric code, stitching quality, label position, carton details and whether the written specification matches the sample. The goal is to approve one bulk-order reference instead of only a showroom impression.

Is a product sample enough without a written specification?

No. The sample and the final written specification should be approved together so the factory and buyer use the same reference for bulk production. A nice sample alone does not prevent production drift.

Should packing data be approved during the sample stage?

Yes. Buyers should confirm carton size, package count, gross weight, CBM and recovery expectations during sample approval because those details affect freight planning, landed cost and the retail launch file.

Can importers skip sample approval on a repeat model?

Repeat orders may reduce sample work, but buyers should still reconfirm any changed fabric, labels, barcode rules, carton marks or destination-market requirements before bulk production. A repeat model can still become a new risk when the commercial setup changes.
