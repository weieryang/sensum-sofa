Compressed Sofa Factory Audit Checklist

Published: 2026-08-31 · Category: Supplier Audit & Quality Control

A quotation shows what a supplier promises. A factory audit tests whether the people, materials, production controls and packing process behind that quotation can deliver the approved product repeatedly. Use this checklist before a first bulk order, a major OEM change or a higher-risk sourcing decision.

Prepare the audit around one sourcing decision

Define the audit scope before the visit or video call. Record the supplier legal name, site address, quoted product family, destination market, expected order volume and the processes you need the factory to control. A compressed sofa project may include foam preparation, fabric cutting, sewing, assembly, recovery testing, vacuum compression, carton packing and export documentation.

Send the agenda in advance, but choose several live records and products during the audit. Prepared samples can explain capability; randomly selected work in progress shows whether controls are used in routine production. Start with the compressed sofa manufacturer selection guide, then use this audit to verify the shortlisted supplier's answers.

Buyer rule: Record what was observed, the document or sample reference, the responsible person and any follow-up required. A checkmark without evidence is not an audit result.

1. Verify supplier identity, site scope and realistic capacity

Match the business identity used for the quotation, contract, payment and export documents. Confirm which production steps occur at the audited site and which are outsourced. Outsourcing is not automatically a failure, but responsibilities, incoming controls and traceability should be visible.

2. Trace foam, fabric and components to the approved model

Select one current or sample model and trace it backward. The product specification should identify the controlled inputs clearly enough for purchasing, warehouse and production teams to use the same version. Ask how substitute materials, color changes and component revisions are approved.

Foam

Check material identification, specified density or support requirement, cutting control, storage condition and the record used for the selected model.

Fabric

Match fabric code, color, approved swatch, roll or lot identity, inspection method and handling of shade variation.

Support components

Verify the frame or internal support route, dimensions, assembly method and any model-specific hardware.

Buyer-supplied artwork

Check revision control for labels, manuals, barcodes, carton marks and private-label files.

Use a written compressed sofa specification sheet as the audit reference. Without a controlled specification, the auditor cannot determine whether the material on the floor is correct.

3. Check production instructions and quality gates

Walk in material-flow order and compare the work being performed with the current instruction. Operators should be able to identify the model and relevant quality point. Look for checks at the stage where a problem can still be corrected, not only a final inspection after all value has been added.

  1. Cutting and preparation: confirm model identification, dimensions, templates and separation of different materials or colors.
  2. Sewing and upholstery: review seam construction, panel orientation, appearance standards and how defects are marked and repaired.
  3. Assembly: verify support alignment, component position, symmetry and any model-specific fastening instruction.
  4. Finished-product check: compare dimensions, appearance, comfort reference and accessories with the approved sample and specification.
  5. Nonconforming product: check identification, segregation, repair approval, reinspection and records for rejected units.

Ask for a recent inspection record and trace one finding to its disposition. For bulk-order sampling and defect language, align the audit with the AQL inspection and defect classification guide.

4. Audit compression, recovery evidence and carton control

Compression packing is a product process, not only a logistics step. Review how the factory sets the compression method, protects upholstery, seals the package, identifies the packed unit and confirms the correct carton. Ask what happens if a bag leaks, a carton dimension changes or a recovery result does not meet the approved reference.

Connect the observations to the compression and recovery test protocol and the site's packing and loading guidance.

5. Confirm export and shipment-release readiness

Review how the supplier turns the buyer's destination requirements into an order file. Product reports and certificates should be checked for holder, manufacturer, model scope, standard, date and destination relevance. Commercial documents, carton marks and quantity records should use consistent parties and product descriptions.

Ask who approves shipment release, which inspection result is required and how packing photos, quantities and corrective actions are closed. Review the certificates and import documents checklist and verify available files on the certificates page. The importer or a qualified compliance adviser should determine which requirements apply in the destination market.

Copy-ready compressed sofa factory audit scorecard

Use a simple evidence scale for each question: 2 = verified and effective, 1 = partially verified or improvement required, and 0 = absent, contradictory or not available. Do not let a total score hide a critical legal, safety, identity or product-conformity risk.

For every open finding, record the risk, required correction, owner, due date and objective closure evidence. The final decision can be approved, conditionally approved, re-audit required or not approved. Keep the audit report with the RFQ, approved sample, specification and contract file.

Compressed sofa factory audit FAQ

Can a compressed sofa factory audit be completed remotely?

A remote audit can verify many documents, live production views and traceability records, but define its limitations. Higher-risk or first orders may justify an on-site or independent audit.

What is the difference between a factory audit and a pre-shipment inspection?

A factory audit evaluates the supplier's systems, capability and controls. A pre-shipment inspection evaluates a specific finished order against agreed requirements before release.

Which evidence matters most during a sofa factory audit?

Useful evidence connects one real model across its specification, material records, production steps, quality checks, compression packing, carton data and finished-goods identity.

Should buyers rely on certificates alone?

No. Check the issuer, holder, product scope, standard, report or certificate number and date, then confirm relevance to the destination market and exact product.

What should happen after the audit?

Classify findings by risk, assign each corrective action to an owner and due date, review objective closure evidence and record the final sourcing decision.
