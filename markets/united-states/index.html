Start with the US sales channel, not only the sofa photo

A compressed sofa sold through a furniture retailer, direct-to-consumer website or marketplace may use the same basic construction but require different package limits, barcode files, delivery instructions, image data and after-sales evidence. Tell the factory where the product will be sold and how the final customer will receive it.

For parcel or threshold delivery, buyers often focus on the dimensions and gross weight of each carton, the number of packages per sellable unit and the risk of one carton arriving separately. For warehouse or LTL programs, pallet logic, carton marks, handling protection and receiving evidence may be more important. These are commercial requirements to lock before final carton approval.

Review 16 CFR Part 1640 against the exact configuration

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission states that 16 CFR Part 1640 applies to in-scope upholstered furniture and incorporates the smolder-resistance methods in TB 117-2013. The methods address cover fabric, resilient filling, barrier material and decking material. A buyer should therefore confirm which tested construction is being used for the ordered model, not simply collect a report from a visually similar sofa.

CPSC also explains that in-scope furniture must carry a permanent English certification label stating: “Complies with U.S. CPSC requirements for upholstered furniture flammability.” The label should be built into the sample and artwork review, rather than added as an unverified last-minute file.

A useful detail for importers: CPSC says a General Certificate of Conformity is not required solely to demonstrate compliance with Part 1640. Other applicable CPSC requirements may create separate testing or certification duties. Keep the product classification and additional components visible when deciding what documentation the importer needs.

Sofa-bed classification: CPSC notes that a detachable mattress portion may fall under mattress flammability rules, while a permanently attached product is likely treated as upholstered furniture. Confirm the final construction before setting the test plan.

Lock permanent labels, country-of-origin marking and retail files

US Customs and Border Protection generally requires imported articles to be marked in a conspicuous, legible and sufficiently permanent way with the English name of the country of origin, unless an exception applies. The importer should confirm the final wording, placement and any repacking process with its customs broker. Review the label on the physical sample and in the packing specification.

Keep regulatory labels separate from retailer and operational files. A marketplace barcode, warehouse label, carton SKU, customer instruction and permanent product label have different purposes. The final approval pack should show each item, its location and the file revision used in production.

Plan cartons around US fulfillment and damage evidence

Request dimensions and gross weight for every carton, not only the total CBM. If one sellable sofa uses two packages, identify them as a set and map which components are inside each carton. This supports freight quoting, warehouse receiving and customer-service handling when one package is delayed or damaged.

Compression can improve container and storage efficiency, but it does not remove the need for corner protection, a controlled vacuum pack, clear opening instructions and a model-specific recovery review. Confirm the acceptable condition and observation time after unpacking during the sample stage.

Use the sample to close both product and US-market files

The sample review should cover appearance, dimensions, seating feel, stitching, function, component count, recovery, labels, instructions and the packed carton. Record accepted changes in a dated specification. A chat message or unnumbered photo is not a reliable bulk-production standard.

If a buyer changes the cover material, filling, barrier, decking, dimensions or construction after testing and sample approval, it should evaluate whether the change affects the compliance file or packing result. Keep the approved material reference and the evidence used for production together.

Inspect the shipment against one revision

Before release, the inspection team should have the same model revision, label artwork, carton file and approved sample reference used by production. The checklist should cover quantity, workmanship, dimensions, function, package count, marks, barcode readability and carton condition. Photos should identify the order and SKU.

US importer FAQ

Does Part 1640 require a GCC for upholstered furniture?

CPSC states that a GCC is not required solely for Part 1640. A permanent certification label is required, while other applicable CPSC rules may create separate certification duties.

What packing data should a US sofa importer request?

Request every carton dimension and gross weight, package count per sellable unit, total CBM, barcode and carton-mark files, packing photos and a loading estimate for the final SKU mix.

Can one flammability report cover every fabric and sofa model?

Do not assume that it can. Confirm the tested construction and material scope against the exact order and current requirement.

What should a US buyer send for a quotation?

Send the model, quantity, destination, channel, fabric and color direction, package limits, label and barcode rules, private-label scope and target date.
